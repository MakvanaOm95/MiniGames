About Dots & Boxes

Dots and Boxes is a classic pencil-and-paper game that people have played in notebooks for more than a hundred years. The board is a grid of dots. Players take turns drawing a single line between two neighbouring dots, across or down.

Whoever draws the fourth side of a box claims that box and must take another turn straight away. When every line has been drawn, the player with the most boxes wins. Here you play against the computer on a 4×4 grid of 16 boxes. It’s a surprisingly sneaky game of planning ahead.

How to play

  1. Press Play. You go first.
  2. Tap between any two neighbouring dots to draw a line.
  3. Complete the fourth side of a box to claim it. You then take another turn.
  4. When all lines are drawn, whoever owns more boxes wins the match.
Controls
Draw a lineTap or click between two dots, or Tab to a line and press Enter
PauseP or Esc, or the pause button
RestartSpace or Enter on the game-over screen

Tips to score higher

  • Never draw the third side. A box with three sides is a free gift for the other player.
  • Count the safe moves. Early on, most lines are safe. Near the end, count how many are left to see who’ll be forced to give boxes away.
  • Look for chains. Boxes in a row can all be taken one after another. Giving away a short chain is better than a long one.
  • Sometimes, give two to get more. Advanced players deliberately leave the last two boxes of a chain so the other player has to open the next, bigger chain.
  • Watch the corners. Corner boxes have only two free sides to start, so they reach three sides quickly.
